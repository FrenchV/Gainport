# Landing-page conversion notes

## Visitor and action

The primary visitor is a Canadian federal prime contractor or program lead who needs senior cloud help on a defined workstream. The primary action is booking a call with Arshad. Email and LinkedIn remain available for visitors who need more information first.

## Research used

- [GOV.UK, Writing for user interfaces](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces): use the visitor's language, reduce cognitive load, and assume many people scan rather than read every word.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, Layer-cake scanning](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/): strong headings help visitors find the part of a page that answers their question.
- [GOV.UK Government Design Principles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/government-design-principles): do less and improve from real usage data.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, Deceptive patterns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/deceptive-patterns/): pressure, misleading choices, and false urgency can harm trust. These were excluded.
- [Baymard, DTC trust research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/dtc-benchmark): visitors may check unfamiliar providers independently. LinkedIn and precise role history offer paths to verify Gainport's claims. Baymard's study concerns consumer commerce, so this is a design inference for a B2B site, not a measured prediction.

## Changes and hypotheses

1. Replaced repeated focus, services, and capability sections with one services section. Hypothesis: visitors can understand the offer and find the booking action faster.
2. Made the main booking action consistent in the header and hero. Hypothesis: fewer competing actions reduce hesitation.
3. Kept specific experience, a LinkedIn link, and compact credentials. Hypothesis: verifiable proof supports trust more than broad claims or decorative cards.
4. Shortened the approach and FAQ. Hypothesis: these answer the main objections without delaying the booking step.
5. Kept the booking calendar on the page and a direct booking-page fallback.

These are research-informed hypotheses, not proven conversion gains. The next useful measurement is the share of visitors who reach the booking section and complete a Calendly booking, segmented by referral source and mobile versus desktop. Review the results and ask a few target visitors where they hesitate before another redesign.
